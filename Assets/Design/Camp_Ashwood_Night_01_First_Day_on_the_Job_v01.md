# CAMP ASHWOOD — NIGHT 01: FIRST DAY ON THE JOB

**Narrative + interactive mission design • v0.1 • 29 October 2026**  
**Platform:** VR • **Players:** 1–4 (same story, variable party size)  
**Target:** 25–30 minutes on a first playthrough; roughly 30 minutes with ordinary exploration.  
**Genre/tone:** Eerie abandoned-camp job simulator that curdles into stylized, playful-but-threatening horror. Dark humor comes from the employer, *not* a cheerful camp environment.

## Continuity — locked premise

There are **three successive periods**, and Night 1 must not explain them to players:

1. **The first Ashwood incident** occurred at the **original/old camp** and led to that site's closure.
2. **The later Camp Ashwood story** (the children, broken-down bus, Mr. Grady, and the journey toward the functioning camp) occurred **after** the original incident.
3. **The present security-guard game** occurs **later still**, when the **newer camp** is also closed. Its wealthy owner has acquired it for demolition and hires a security team to stop vandalism, theft, and souvenir hunters.

The players are assigned to the **newer, now-abandoned camp**; they are **not** starting at the original camp. Evidence of the first incident and the children's story is background to uncover over multiple nights, not the owner's Night 1 exposition. The precise connections among the later murders, Grady, and the newer camp's closure are not yet locked. If a document or prop is ambiguous, keep it ambiguous instead of establishing accidental canon.

## Core creative rules

- **Ashwood is not fun; people find fun in leftover working equipment.** Daylight reveals dust and disrepair. After dark, it feels occupied.
- **The boss is a slimy 1980s cheapskate**, fast-talking, self-interested, petty, entertaining and unhelpfully confident. He is not a stand-up comedian; don't have him undercut *every* scare. His levity should make sudden concern more potent.
- **Security work creates the route**, not a glowing waypoint tour. Instructions are job requests delivered by walkie, with a physical paper map/clipboard.
- **No monsters and no combat tutorial on Night 1.** Threat is suggested; this is not a death/chase level.
- **Scares have physical causes or deliberately unresolved outcomes.** Use one sharp sting for a plausible accident; prioritize unscored one-time sounds. The final event is authored.
- **Build to solve a problem, not to demonstrate an editor.** A cabin repair is a meaningful security task and a setup for its later violation.
- **1–4 player safe:** shared story beats and world state; individual quiet cues are optional; neither the owner nor main objectives assumes exactly two people.
- **Use visual clue → audio cue → useful player action.** The environment should direct attention before the owner repeats an instruction.

## Owner — working character sheet

**Working name:** Martin “Marty” Bixby, property buyer and remote dispatcher (name not yet canon).  
**Voice:** Confident, nasal, performatively friendly sales pitch; little bursts of irritation; half whispers about liability; speaks in short, memorable bursts. Does not do overt villain monologues.  
**Believes:** The property is a miserable asset that needs to be secured cheaply until demolition. Guards should not get hurt—or, more accurately, cause lawsuits.  
**His mask:** “You kids are lucky I hired you.”  
**Humanizing detail:** He knows the map and electrical system from contractor notes; can occasionally give genuinely helpful instructions. When alarmed, he drops his salesman's rhythm.  
**Limits:** He only knows what people report, what he has in paperwork, and potentially what cameras/alarms genuinely cover. He is not clairvoyant.

**Performance note:** Keep most radio bursts 4–12 seconds; the opening briefing can be 30–45 seconds divided naturally across movement. Default radio traffic should not block movement. Use separate takes for a solo guard (“you”) versus a team (“you people/you guys”) as necessary. Trigger-based variations avoid expensive full branching conversations.

## Playable area and spatial loop

The Night 1 route is a **small, memorable horseshoe**, approximately one to two minutes' walk end-to-end without investigation:

**Entrance / welcome sign → Security office → Maintenance shed & generator → Cabin Row A (Cabin 6) → recreation court / dining hall edge / lake overlook → security office.**

The *old Ashwood site* is beyond a blocked/overgrown spur and is not accessible on Night 1. Other newer-camp buildings and the deeper lake are readable from a distance, but not part of the critical route. Use physical closures, bad footing, broken bridges, and temporary work orders—not arbitrary invisible walls.

**Initial look:** peeling camp paint, damp boards, dust, leaves, missing letters on signs, silent speaker poles, a basketball with little air, a working but unstable generator. The world is quiet enough that a simple tap sounds suspicious. No cheerful musical score.

## Thirty-minute mission progression

> Times are expected playtime beats, *not* hard cutscene timers. Each beat has a player action and an event-driven exit condition. Fast players can finish closer to 25 minutes; exploration and co-op chatter naturally fill ~30. Avoid artificially making players wait for a clock.

### 00:00–03:30 — Sequence 1: The Gate / “Welcome to Paradise”

**Player sees:** Faded `CAMP ASHWOOD` sign, a chain loosely looped on the gate, flaking “PRIVATE PROPERTY / DEMOLITION PENDING” notice, fresh cigarette butts or boot prints. The cabins are distant behind a dark treeline. The gates are not a jump scare.

**Actions:** Walk to the gate, lift the chain, open the gate; find the security office by following a worn directional sign and the owner's call. One optional inspection (notice or old camp photo on a post) gives texture, not a plot reveal.

**Radio VO, first connection:**

- **BIXBY / VO-01 (autoplay at entrance):** “Testing. Testing. Hello? Ah, there we go. Welcome to Camp Ashwood, the most expensive mistake I ever bought at a discount.”
- **VO-02 (after players open gate):** “I'm Marty Bixby. I own this charming pile of lumber, and for tonight, you're my highly trained security division. Don't let that title go to your heads.”
- **VO-03 (walking toward office):** “Here's the business model: I tear it down, somebody builds something profitable, and in the meantime you keep the local treasure hunters from walking off with the doorknobs.”
- **VO-04 (conditional if they inspect demolition notice):** “Yep. Demolition. The land's worth more than the buildings, which is a sentence I should've checked before I signed anything.”

**Systems:** VR movement, interactable gate, radio trigger/VO, ambient audio, non-critical inspectable clues, party join synchronization.

**Exit:** At least one player enters security-office trigger; group is oriented nearby or can catch up at will.

### 03:30–07:00 — Sequence 2: Security Office / “The Rules Are More Like Suggestions”

**Player sees:** Unlit office with dust motes, paper map, clipboard, locker containing flashlights, an old logbook, wall key hooks. Work order bears a current date and a crudely stamped `TEMPORARY SECURITY` label. Optional photo of *this newer camp* in healthier days, without names or timeline revelations.

**Actions:** Collect flashlight(s), physically open the property map, take or interact with the assignment clipboard, perform a radio check-in. Let players inspect a small radio and markers; not everyone must manipulate everything. Paper-based guidance remains accessible.

**Required work orders shown:**

1. Restore power to the **security-side circuit** at the maintenance shed.
2. Secure **Cabin 6's damaged door opening** with available lumber.
3. Inspect **recreation/dining-hall exterior** and report an unsecured access point.
4. Return and complete a shift log.

**Radio VO:**

- **VO-05:** “That's your office. Fancy, right? Comes with four walls and absolutely no heating bill. Do *not* switch on the heater; the electrician says it builds character by catching fire.”
- **VO-06:** “Take a flashlight. Take the map. There's a generator behind the maintenance shed. Get that working and the security-side lights should come on. I emphasize *should*.”
- **VO-07 (after check-in):** “One other thing. People ask about the place's history. There was some ugly business a few years back. That's why it's empty. You're not here to write a documentary. You're here to keep people from stealing copper.”
- **VO-08 (if a player asks/reports history item):** “I bought a deed, not a scrapbook. Focus on the buildings that are still standing.”

**Systems:** Flashlight, inventory/equipment, diegetic map, shared objective tracker, context VO, inspectable archival props.

**Exit:** Flashlight acquired and work order viewed by at least one team member; generator task becomes active. No cinematic lock-in.

### 07:00–13:30 — Sequence 3: Maintenance Shed & Generator / “Do Not Electrocute Yourselves”

**Player sees:** Workshop covered in grit; one old cabinet contains a replacement fuse, plus gloves and tools. Generator has an empty/loose fuel feed and a breaker panel. Exterior maintenance floodlights are dark.

**Actions:** Locate cabinet, take fuse, identify fuel can already in shed, attach or pour fuel (one simple interaction), insert fuse into breaker box, pull generator starter, then flip **SECURITY** breaker. Make the steps tactile and causally readable; avoid a long scavenger hunt. One owner hint if no progress after meaningful exploration, not a repeating nag.

**Scare SE-01 — Falling bucket (optional but common):** While a player is rummaging in cabinet, a metal bucket/tin falls from a *different* shelf behind them. **One** brief sting, hard metal impact, then ordinary ringing decay. All nearby players see the fallen object. It stays on the floor. Do not instantly explain it. Use a cooldown so no other startle fires for several minutes.

**Radio VO:**

- **VO-09:** “Okay, easy job. Find the fuse. Feed that antique dinosaur some fuel. Pull the starter. Then switch on the breaker marked SECURITY. In that order. I paid good money to have those labels put on.”
- **VO-10 (fallback after 80–110 sec with no progress):** “Cabinet, left side of the shed. Little fuse box. If it's still there, congratulations—we've beaten the scavengers.”
- **VO-11 (if player calls about crash):** “Something fell? In a fifty-year-old shed? Call the newspapers. Check no one got hurt and keep going.”
- **VO-12 (successful ignition):** “Hey! Listen to that! She lives. The contractor told me that thing was scrap. Remind me to argue about his invoice.”
- **VO-13 (security lights switch on):** “There. Lights. See? Already making the place look less like a crime scene. Well, marginally.”

**World change:** Nearby yard lamps and security-office lights come on in sequence; a remote recreation-hall sign briefly buzzes, but its area is *not* on the requested circuit. Treat as an ambiguous transient effect; no confirmation yet that it's supernatural.

**Systems:** Object inspection, resource collection, simple tool/slot use, generator state machine, switch/power-zone activation, persistent physical scare prop, spatial horror audio, radio reports, objective state.

**Exit:** Generator online *and* security breaker on. Fallback if player throws wrong breaker: clear click/label feedback and a resettable state; no softlock.

### 13:30–18:30 — Sequence 4: Cabin 6 / “A Door Is Not a Suggestion”

**Player sees:** One cabin with a pried-open door and a gap near the threshold. Boot marks, dust knocked from a shelf, old bunk bed silhouettes through dirty glass. No overt monsters or bodies. A **different** cabin farther down Row A remains locked.

**Actions:** Collect 2–3 available boards and fasteners from a nearby material stack (or carry from shed); use build tool to place boards against a designated structural frame. A ghost preview shows valid fit, aligned by face/bounds and 0.5m build lattice. Let players vary how they cover the opening. “Secure” completes when sufficient opening coverage + a minimum attachment/support threshold is met; not when they mimic a hand-authored exact configuration. In co-op, one player can fetch supplies while another builds.

**Optional activity:** A dusty basketball near the recreation court can be bounced or shot later—never a quest and never accompanied by cheerful reward music.

**Radio VO:**

- **VO-14 (on arriving):** “Cabin Six. My favorite. Only one that came with the door pre-removed. Some idiot's been prying it open for souvenirs.”
- **VO-15 (build introduction):** “There's scrap lumber in the rack by the path. Fix that opening so someone can't just waltz inside. Make it ugly. We're tearing it down anyway.”
- **VO-16 (first placed board):** “Look at that, actual craftsmanship. You keep this up, I'll have you build me a house. On your own time, obviously.”
- **VO-17 (complete):** “That'll hold. Or at least it'll slow down anybody who doesn't own a crowbar.”
- **VO-18 (if player builds extra cover):** “Whoa. Are we securing a cabin or building a bank vault? Easy on my lumber, Rockefeller.”

**Optional discoverable history:** One dusty photo/trophy of **the newer, occupied camp**; its logo is modernized from an older hand-painted original visible in a tucked-away maintenance inventory. Neither the boss nor task UI mentions Grady or the previous incidents. A small notation `OLD SITE — DO NOT USE` may appear on a faded folded grounds map; no explanation and no need to find it.

**Systems:** Grid/build placement, socket/face attachment, material inventory, coverage-based structural assessment, co-op authority, persistent construction states, owner reaction to action.

**Exit:** Opening passes security validation and at least one player reports it through the work order/radio.

### 18:30–23:00 — Sequence 5: Perimeter Walk / “Eyes Open, Mouth Shut”

**Player sees:** Dusk turns quickly to blue-black. Security floodlights create islands of illumination; beyond the lit perimeter are silent paths and an unused archery lane. Dining hall doors, rec court, and lake overlook are visible. The camp feels EMPTY, not inviting.

**Actions:** Inspect and report dining-hall exterior latch; check a marked gate padlock or broken fence section; pass the recreation court where some old objects *still work*. Optional fishing rod at a near-shore kiosk—allow a few casts, no quest requirement. The owner can react to power use or leisure but never demands it. Report a plainly explainable sign of entry (fresh discarded wrapper, forced staple, wet boot mark) without concluding its origin.

**Scare SE-02 — One-time footsteps (optional):** A lone player within an eligible short path hears two steps parallel to them, then one on a wooden walkway. No sting and no repeat. For co-op, other players may not hear it; never require a report.

**Scare SE-03 — Exterior false alarm (conditional; max one):** A loose sheet of metal bangs, then an animal dashes from brush at a safe distance. This can fire if the quiet scare did not; avoid creating a guaranteed parade of scares. No musical swell after reveal.

**Radio VO:**

- **VO-19:** “Almost done. Check the dining-hall latch, then circle back past the recreation court. If somebody's been in there, make a note. Don't go playing detective.”
- **VO-20 (if power lit the rec equipment):** “Oh, the court lights work? Great. Maybe I can sell those separately.”
- **VO-21 (if a player shoots basketball / fishes):** “Sure, take a break. I'm only paying for a security shift, not Olympic training. Keep an eye on the fence while you're at it.”
- **VO-22 (on reported entry signs):** “Yeah, exactly why I hired you. Folks want a little piece of spooky-camp history. I'll settle for them not stealing the plumbing.”
- **VO-23 (on report of footsteps):** “You *saw* somebody? No? Then write down 'noise,' not 'intruder.' Big difference on an insurance form.”

**Systems:** Dynamic time/lighting, environmental soundscape, patrol inspection, optional physics activities, radio report options, individualized scare routing, shared evidence log.

**Exit:** Dining-hall latch status checked, secondary perimeter check done, and owner sends group back toward Row A/security office.

### 23:00–27:30 — Sequence 6: Cabin 6 / “Three Knocks”

**Player sees:** The newly repaired cabin lies along the normal return route. The power lights throw its boarded opening into sharp relief. Important: the game remembers *their* boards and keeps their exact orientation.

**Actions:** As the last nearby player passes the trigger (without requiring exact eye direction), three deliberate knocks come from *inside* Cabin 6. Give time to look, approach, and voluntarily report. No music under the first knocks. If the group tries to leave immediately, the owner can ask for status and the story still proceeds.

**Radio VO:**

- **VO-24 (if player reports knocks):** “Something knocking? Probably boards settling. Which cabin?”
- **VO-25 (player selects Cabin 6 / location inferred):** “...Six?” [a short, real silence] “Okay. You secured that one, right?”
- **VO-26 (after confirmed repair):** “Good. Then don't take it apart just to satisfy your curiosity. Head back to the office.”

**The impact:** After the group has had a fair chance to orient toward the cabin—or a short bounded grace period—an unseen force hits the inner face of the repaired opening once. Use a **single powerful wooden impact with a short restrained horror accent**. Boards flex in their placed locations; dust falls; fasteners rattle. The repair remains mostly intact, preserving evidence. If the opening is insufficiently boarded owing to an alternate completion path, slam an interior door and throw an outward puff of dust instead—do not fake interaction with non-existent boards.

- **VO-27 (after impact, sober, immediate):** “Nope. Don't touch it. Back to the office. Now.”
- **VO-28 (if players linger):** “I'm serious. We can send somebody in daylight. I am not explaining an injury to an attorney tonight.”

The boss returns to terse practical language. He does not know the explanation, and there is no monster reveal. Players *can* look at the cabin and talk about it; do not pin them in place, teleport them, or force a cutscene. A subtle wayfinder is the row of yard lamps they themselves powered.

**Systems:** Persistent construction queries, authored scare state machine, synchronized physical animation, adaptive impact presentation, walkie report and response, optional proximity behavior, nightly progression gate.

**Exit:** Cabin scare completes and players return to the office zone; no need to maintain everyone's proximity at the scare location.

### 27:30–30:00 — Sequence 7: Shift Log / “Write It Down”

**Player sees:** Security office lamps buzzing. Shift log has the earlier tasks checked off plus a blank `UNEXPLAINED ACTIVITY` line. An old PA speaker somewhere in the grounds makes one tiny electrical pop while the rest of the camp is quiet—*not* a new jump scare.

**Actions:** File a short report using physical clipboard choices, e.g. `STRUCTURAL NOISE`, `POSSIBLE TRESPASS`, `UNKNOWN`. The choice is recorded and influences the boss's immediate line / later follow-ups, but does not alter the fact that the event occurred. Place flashlight on rack if desired. Shared shift completion, no competitive scoring.

**Radio VO:**

- **VO-29 (office arrival):** “Everybody back? Good. Now fill out the incident line. Properly. If that building collapses, I'd like the paperwork to say it was already thinking about it.”
- **VO-30A (report = structural noise):** “That's the sensible answer. Old buildings make old-building noises.”
- **VO-30B (report = possible trespass):** “Fine. I'll have somebody check the locks tomorrow. In daylight. With a hard hat.”
- **VO-30C (report = unknown):** “'Unknown.' Beautiful. Very reassuring. You really know how to make an owner feel great about his investment.”
- **VO-31 (closing; no quip after this):** “You did the job. Don't go back out there tonight. I'll call you in the morning.”

A little too much silence. A quiet **three-tap pattern** from an indeterminate distance *may* recur as a coda only if restrained enough not to turn the cabin incident into an obvious supernatural musical theme. Prefer to end on unnerving silence for v0.1.

**End card:** `NIGHT 1 COMPLETE` / `FIRST DAY ON THE JOB`.

**Systems:** Diegetic daily report, shared mission completion, journal/world-state persistence, final VO branching, progression to later night.

## VO trigger plan (not every line must play)

| Channel | Event | Listener scope | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening guided VO | Gate/open office | All | One voice shared on each player's walkie; slightly compressed radio band |
| Job instructions | New objective/first approach | All | No instruction spam; one reminder after a real stall |
| Ambient boss banter | Optional activity or creative build | All or nearby | Short, suppressible while active scare or team comms |
| Player radio report | Explicit report interaction | All | Responses based on *reported* fact, not owner's omniscience |
| Individual hallucination cue | Short footsteps | One eligible player | No boss reply unless a player reports it |
| Major story scare | Cabin impact | All within realistic audibility | World event synchronized; remote players hear through environment as appropriate |

**Voice chat integration:** Boss VO ducks slightly against active player speech but remains intelligible. Allow replay through a radio log/clipboard transcript instead of repeating the scare. A solo player can complete every task.

## Systems highlighted and acceptance criteria

| System | Night 1 use | Prototype acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| **Narrative/quest director** | Routes work orders and finale | Every task works independently of player count; no timer softlocks |
| **Walkie/owner dialogue** | Guided brief, reactive lines, incident reports | Player can trigger report, get matching response, and read missed VO |
| **XR interactions** | Gates, map, cabinet, fuse, fuel, starter, breaker, log | All reachable with controller interactions; clear confirmation |
| **Inventory/resources** | Fuse, fuel, boards/fasteners, flashlight | No loss/duplication; items can be carried or redeemed reliably |
| **Building/placement** | Board repair on Cabin 6 | Face/bounds alignment + 0.5m quantization; multi-approach valid solution |
| **Security assessment** | Detect cabin opening is secured | Checks coverage and support; no exact-piece placement dependency |
| **Power & environment** | Generator and security circuit | Lamps, building cues, and failures respond to state |
| **Time/lighting** | Dusk-to-night during shift | Key locations readable with flashlights; night feels dark and eerie |
| **Scare event director** | Bucket, footsteps/animal, cabin finale | At most 1–2 optional notable scares; authored finale once; cooldown and state persistence |
| **Spatial/audio** | Object fall, footsteps, radio, cabin hit | Physical audio localized; stingers brief and not dangerously loud |
| **Co-op authority** | Shared objects, builds, reports, physical events | Host/server governs world state; optional psychological cues can be local |
| **Save/world memory** | What was repaired, which scares fired, what was reported | Night 2 can reflect Night 1, and late co-op joins receive correct state |

## Guardrails for direction and testing

- **No premature lore reveal:** Avoid saying “Grady,” explaining the bus, or recounting either old camp event in Night 1 mandatory dialogue. One subtle `OLD SITE` artifact is enough.
- **Do not label these as 'ghost' or 'haunted' in objectives.** People inside the fiction assume vandals, animals, or failing construction.
- **No easy instant explanation of the cabin impact.** But preserve ambiguity: it might be an intruder, animal, or something worse.
- **Avoid cutscene control grabs** in VR; players may look away. Build audio, position, and physical evidence to carry the moment.
- **Playtest for 25–30 minutes** with complete newcomers; measure task completion and actual travel/inspection, not just intended script duration.
- **Accessibility:** Comfortable sound-level ceiling, subtitles, clear directional audio option, seated/reach alternatives for low cabinet objects, scene-safe flashlight levels, scare intensity preference that softens stings without removing narrative cues.

## Questions deliberately not locked

- Is “Marty Bixby” the final name, or do we want a more memorable boss identity?
- How directly does he know the truth about the newer camp's shutdown? Night 1 only implies he knows more than the property listing.
- Which specific original Ashwood artifact connects to later nights? Leave the timeline and Grady reveal for narrative mapping before authoring props.
- Are there larger risks in Night 2 onward (trespassers, creature encounters, combat), and how does the work assignment evolve?

**Night 1 intended aftertaste:** “We fixed the lights, we secured that door, and something hit it from *inside*. He was making jokes all night. Why did he stop?”
